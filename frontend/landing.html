[ 01 / How it works ]Figure 1 — one run, end to end

From a URL to a liability report, without writing a single test.

01 — Observe

Reads the page like a person

Every screen becomes a numbered list of what can be clicked, typed or chosen, the text actually on screen, and a screenshot.

02 — Decide

Thinks in character

The persona's brain picks one action, in its own voice, within its limits — a senior avoids unlabeled icons, a commuter skims.

03 — Act

Uses a real browser

Playwright clicks and fills for real, then times whether anything visibly changed before the persona's patience ran out.

04 — Feel

Gets frustrated, and leaves

Confusion, silence after a click and vague errors raise frustration. Past the threshold the persona quits — that is your drop-off.

[ 03 / Engines ]Friction and misuse · tested in the same run

One engine finds where people get stuck. The other finds where money leaks.

Empathy engine

Where real people get stuck.

Runs the novice and the commuter. Half of what it reports is measured by the browser itself, so it holds up when a developer pushes back.

  • MeasuredClicks with no visible response, timed against each persona's patience
  • MeasuredButtons covered by banners and pop-ups, disabled controls, blocked form submits
  • MeasuredContrast, missing labels and alt text, tiny tap targets, sideways scrolling
  • MeasuredSlow pages and slow responses on the persona's own network
  • AI-observedA vision model reviews every new screen through the persona's eyes
Personas: Ravi · PriyaScores usability · accessibility · performance
Integrity engine

Where your business logic leaks.

Runs the Deal-Hunter. Each abuse is attempted for real, then a strict judge compares the page before and after — and only reports a violation with concrete evidence.

  • CouponsThe same code twice, several codes stacked
  • QuantitiesZero, negative and absurd quantities
  • PricesHidden or read-only price fields edited in the browser
  • FlowJumping straight to the confirmation page without paying
  • RepeatsDouble-submitting payment, submitting again after going back

Runs only after you confirm you own or are authorised to test the site, and never leaves that site.

[ 05 / FAQ ]Five questions developers ask first

Before you point it at your site.

Do I need to write test scripts or selectors?

No. You give a URL and a task in plain English. Each persona plans its own path, finds elements by what they say on screen, and fills whole forms in a single step — names, dates, dropdowns, checkboxes.

Is this just an LLM's opinion of my site?

No. Most findings are measured by the browser: response timing against a persona's patience, overlays blocking clicks, contrast ratios, missing labels, failed requests, duplicate submissions. AI-observed findings are labelled as such, capped at medium severity unless confirmed by a second persona, and count half towards the score.

Can I point it at my production site?

The Empathy Engine behaves like a normal visitor, so yes. The Integrity Engine deliberately tries to abuse business rules, so it only runs after you confirm you own or are authorised to test the site — use staging for it.

What do I get at the end?

A liability report: a score per vital, an executive summary with a ship / fix-first verdict, every persona's step-by-step journey, and the deduplicated findings with screenshots and fixes. Each run is saved, can be replayed in the dashboard, and is exported as a PDF.

How long does a run take?

About one and a half minutes for three personas on a typical sign-up or checkout flow, because they run in parallel.
